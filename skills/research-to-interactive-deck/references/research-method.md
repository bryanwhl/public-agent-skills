# Research method

## Source hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order, while adapting to the domain:

1. Standards, laws, official documentation, original datasets, and first-party technical material
2. Peer-reviewed papers and reputable institutional publications
3. High-quality books, university teaching material, and expert analysis
4. Reputable journalism for events, context, and competing viewpoints
5. Community sources for practical experience, clearly labeled as anecdotal

Use secondary sources to discover primary material, not as automatic substitutes for it.

## Research loop

1. Write the learner questions before searching.
2. Gather one overview source to establish terminology and boundaries.
3. Find primary evidence for each important claim.
4. Search for disagreement, limitations, and failure cases.
5. Recheck dates and versions for facts that may have changed.
6. Stop when every learner question is answered with sufficient evidence and additional sources mostly repeat known information.

## Claim ledger

Track the following while working:

| Claim | Evidence URL | Publisher | Published/updated | Accessed | Confidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Do not expose the full ledger in the main presentation. Use it to produce precise inline citation markers and a useful source list.

## Evidence rules

- Cite numbers, dates, direct attributions, disputed claims, and claims that may change.
- Cite the nearest supporting source rather than a generic homepage.
- Do not cite a source that merely mentions the subject without supporting the claim.
- Avoid citation laundering: trace claims through summaries to the original evidence when practical.
- If credible sources disagree, explain the disagreement and why rather than silently choosing one.
- Label extrapolations and synthesized conclusions as inference.
- Use short paraphrases. Quote only when wording itself matters.

## Scope control

Build the smallest coherent curriculum that reaches the user's goal. Move advanced branches into expandable detail or a "Where to go next" section. Exclude interesting material that does not strengthen the learner's mental model.
